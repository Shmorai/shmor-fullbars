How fast can each connection go?

Every broadband deal sits on one of these technologies. The bars show the typical ceiling of each one, so you can spot a “fibre” deal that isn’t really fibre.

ADSL (copper)

up to ~24 Mbps

The oldest broadband: phone-line copper all the way to your home. Speed falls the further you are from the exchange.

Being phased out

Part fibre (FTTC / VDSL)

up to ~80 Mbps

Fibre to the street cabinet, then copper to your home. Sold as “fibre broadband” in the UK, but it isn’t full fibre.

Common in the UK

4G / 5G home broadband

varies a lot

A router with a SIM inside, using a mobile network. No line needed, but speed depends on local signal and busy times.

No engineer needed

Satellite (low orbit)

tens to hundreds of Mbps

Services like Starlink use low-orbit satellites. Useful where nothing else reaches; needs a clear view of the sky.

Rural option

Cable (DOCSIS)

1 Gbps+ download

Coaxial cable networks such as Virgin Media, Xfinity and Spectrum. Very fast downloads, slower uploads.

Big in the US

Full fibre (FTTP / FTTH)

1 Gbps to multi-gig

Fibre optic cable all the way into your home. The fastest and most reliable option, with matching upload speeds on many plans.

Fastest

Switch in four steps

In the UK, most broadband and mobile switches are now led by your new provider.

Full switching guide →

  1. Check your end date

    Your provider must tell you before your contract ends. Leaving early can mean exit fees.

  2. Check your address

    Use the provider’s checker and the Ofcom checker for speeds and coverage.

  3. Read the contract summary

    Price, length, any price rises in pounds and pence, and the minimum speed promised.

  4. Let the new provider switch you

    One Touch Switch for broadband, or text “PAC” to 65075 to keep your mobile number.

Frequently asked questions

Is FullBars a broadband or mobile provider?
No. FullBars is a free comparison and guide site. You sign up directly with the provider you choose, under their terms. Some links may be affiliate links, which never changes what we write.
Which countries does FullBars cover?
We focus on the UK and also cover the USA and Europe, with region guides for US internet and cell plans and European broadband and mobile.
Why don’t you show prices on every card?
Broadband and mobile prices change weekly and vary by address, so a price on a card would quickly be wrong. We show the facts that stay true (network, technology, owner, contract types) and send you to the provider to check today’s price for your address.
What does “FullBars” mean?
Full signal bars: the connection you want. Our job is to help you find a provider that gives you full bars where you live, at a price that makes sense.
