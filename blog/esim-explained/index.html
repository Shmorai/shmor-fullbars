eSIM explained

An eSIM is a SIM built into your phone and downloaded over the air. It makes switching networks and adding travel data quicker, and some newer phones no longer have a physical SIM slot.

By the FullBars editorial team · 28 September 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: User_Pascal on Unsplash

On this page

  1. How an eSIM works
  2. Which phones support it
  3. Pros and cons
  4. Switching to an eSIM provider

How an eSIM works

Instead of inserting a plastic card, you scan a QR code or use your network’s app to download a profile to your phone. Most eSIM phones can store several profiles and use one or two at a time.

Which phones support it

Recent iPhones, Google Pixels, Samsung Galaxy phones and many other models support eSIM, as do some smartwatches and tablets. Some US iPhone models are eSIM-only. Check your phone’s settings or the manufacturer’s site.

Pros and cons

  • Pros: instant activation, easy dual-SIM, no waiting for post, handy travel eSIMs.
  • Cons: moving to a new phone means transferring or re-downloading the profile; not every provider supports eSIM on every plan.

Switching to an eSIM provider

Your number-keeping rights are the same. In the UK, text PAC to 65075 and give the code to your new provider. In the US, ask the new carrier to port your number.
