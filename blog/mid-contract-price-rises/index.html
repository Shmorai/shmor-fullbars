Mid-contract price rises: the pounds-and-pence rule

For years, UK broadband and mobile bills rose every April by inflation plus a few per cent. Ofcom changed the rules, so you can now see exactly how much your bill will rise before you sign.

The rule in short

Since 17 January 2025, Ofcom has banned UK phone, broadband and pay-TV providers from including inflation-linked or percentage-based price rise terms in new contracts. Any price rises written into a contract must be set out clearly in pounds and pence at the point of sale, so you know the cost in advance.

What it means when you compare deals

  • Look in the contract summary for the scheduled rise, usually each April.
  • Add up the total cost over the whole minimum term, including those rises.
  • Two deals with the same starting price can cost different amounts over 24 months.

Can I leave if my price goes up?

If a price rise was clearly set out in your contract, you generally can’t leave penalty-free because of it. But if your provider raises prices in a way that wasn’t clearly set out, or changes terms to your detriment, you may have the right to leave without paying exit fees. You must normally be given at least one month’s notice of such a change.

Older contracts

The rule applies to contracts taken out from January 2025. If you signed earlier, your contract may still include inflation-linked or percentage rises. Providers that signed the government’s Telecoms Consumer Charter agreed that April 2026 would be the last time they apply inflation-linked increases. When your contract ends, compare a new deal.

Note that the rule makes rises clear, but doesn’t cap them: providers still set their own prices.

Worked example: comparing two 24-month deals

Imagine two broadband deals with the same starting monthly price. Deal A has a rise of a set number of pounds each April written into the contract; Deal B has a smaller rise. Over a 24-month contract that starts in, say, October, you would usually pay two April rises, so the total cost can differ noticeably even though the advertised price was identical. Always multiply each price by the number of months you will pay it and add any set-up fees.

Where to find the price rise in your contract

Providers must give you a short contract summary before you sign. Look for the section on prices: it should state any annual rise in pounds and pence and when it applies. If a deal page shows only the starting price, ask for the contract summary before you commit.

Social tariffs and price rises

If you receive Universal Credit or certain other benefits, a social tariff may be cheaper than a standard deal, and many have short or no minimum terms. Check each provider’s terms for how prices change.

When your contract ends

Your provider must tell you, before your minimum term ends, that it is ending and what its best deals are. Out-of-contract prices are often higher, so that is the time to compare. Switching broadband is easier now thanks to One Touch Switch.

Frequently asked questions

Are mid-contract price rises banned completely?
No. Ofcom banned inflation-linked and percentage-based rise terms in new contracts from 17 January 2025. Providers can still include rises, but they must be set out in pounds and pence when you sign.
Does the rule apply to mobile contracts?
Yes. It applies to mobile, broadband, landline and pay-TV contracts taken out from 17 January 2025.
Can I leave if my provider adds a rise that was not in my contract?
Usually yes. If a provider changes the price or terms in a way that was not clearly set out and is to your detriment, you can generally leave without exit fees. You should get at least one month’s notice.
