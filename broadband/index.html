Broadband types, by top speed

ADSL (copper)

up to ~24 Mbps

The oldest broadband: phone-line copper all the way to your home. Speed falls the further you are from the exchange.

Being phased out

Part fibre (FTTC / VDSL)

up to ~80 Mbps

Fibre to the street cabinet, then copper to your home. Sold as “fibre broadband” in the UK, but it isn’t full fibre.

Common in the UK

4G / 5G home broadband

varies a lot

A router with a SIM inside, using a mobile network. No line needed, but speed depends on local signal and busy times.

How UK broadband is built

Most UK homes are connected by Openreach, the BT Group company that runs the national phone and broadband network. BT, Sky, TalkTalk, Vodafone, EE, Plusnet, NOW and many smaller providers rent Openreach lines and sell their own packages on top. That’s why two providers on the same network can offer the same top speed at your address.

Virgin Media runs its own separate cable and fibre network. And a growing group of altnets (alternative networks) such as CityFibre, Hyperoptic, Community Fibre and others are building new full fibre networks, either selling broadband themselves or letting providers like Vodafone, TalkTalk and Zen use them.

What to compare (beyond price)

  • Technology: full fibre (FTTP), part fibre (FTTC), cable or mobile. This decides the speed ceiling.
  • Minimum guaranteed speed: under Ofcom’s voluntary code, providers give you an estimated speed and a minimum. If speeds fall below it and can’t be fixed, you may be able to leave without a penalty.
  • Contract length: 12, 18 or 24 months is common. Check the total cost over the whole term, not just the first month.
  • Price rises: since January 2025, any mid-contract rises must be set out in pounds and pence at the point of sale. Read our guide.
  • Upload speed: important for video calls, cloud backups and creators. Full fibre often offers far higher uploads than part fibre or cable.
  • Router and wi-fi: mesh or wi-fi extenders can matter more than the line speed in a big home.
  • Set-up costs and landline: some deals include a phone line, others are broadband-only.

How to check what you can get

Your exact address decides everything. Use the provider’s own availability checker and the independent Ofcom broadband and mobile checker to see which technologies reach your home. If full fibre is available, compare a few providers on the same network as well as altnets.

Moving home? Order broadband as soon as you have a move date. New full fibre connections may need an engineer visit.

Your rights as a UK broadband customer

  • End-of-contract notices: your provider must tell you when your minimum term is ending and what its best deals are.
  • Easy switching: under One Touch Switch, your new provider arranges the move, even between different networks.
  • Automatic compensation: providers in Ofcom’s scheme pay set amounts if a repair or new line is late, or an engineer misses an appointment.
  • Complaints: if a complaint isn’t resolved after eight weeks, you can take it to the provider’s ombudsman scheme for free.

Frequently asked questions

Is “fibre broadband” the same as full fibre?
Not always. In the UK “fibre” has often meant fibre to the street cabinet (FTTC), with copper for the last stretch. Full fibre (FTTP) runs fibre right into your home and is much faster. Look for “full fibre” or “FTTP”.
Which UK broadband provider is the fastest?
Speed depends on the network at your address more than the brand. Full fibre providers on Openreach, Virgin Media’s network and altnets can all offer gigabit speeds where they reach. Check what technology serves your home first.
Do I need a landline for broadband?
No. Many full fibre and cable deals are broadband-only, and the UK is moving landlines to digital calls over broadband anyway. See our home phone guide.
