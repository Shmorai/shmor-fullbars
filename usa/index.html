In the US, home internet choice depends heavily on your address, with cable, fiber, fixed wireless and satellite competing street by street. Cell service is run by three national carriers and many prepaid brands.

Home internet types in the US

  • Cable (Xfinity, Spectrum, Cox): widely available in cities and suburbs, with fast downloads.
  • Fiber (AT&T Fiber, Verizon Fios and others): the fastest option, with symmetrical speeds on many plans, where available.
  • 5G home internet (T-Mobile, Verizon, AT&T Internet Air): simple setup, no line needed; performance depends on local signal.
  • Satellite (Starlink and others): for rural areas where wired options don’t reach.

Check the FCC National Broadband Map to see which providers report service at your address.

Use the FCC broadband label

Since 2024, internet providers must show a standard “broadband facts” label at the point of sale, listing the monthly price, introductory periods, one-time and monthly fees, and typical speeds and latency. It’s the easiest way to compare like with like. Read our guide.

Cell plans: postpaid vs prepaid

Postpaid plans from AT&T, Verizon and T-Mobile bundle perks and often device deals, billed after use. Prepaid brands (Cricket, Visible, Metro, Mint and others) are paid in advance and are often cheaper for the same network. Check whether a prepaid brand has lower data priority at busy times.

Help with costs

The federal Lifeline program offers a monthly discount on phone or internet service for eligible low-income households. The Affordable Connectivity Program stopped taking new funding in 2024. Some providers also run their own low-income plans. Check eligibility at the Lifeline Support site.

Frequently asked questions

What counts as broadband in the US?
In 2024 the FCC raised its benchmark for fixed broadband to 100 Mbps download and 20 Mbps upload.
How do I keep my number when switching carrier?
Ask the new carrier to port your number. You’ll need your account number and a number transfer PIN from your current carrier. Don’t cancel the old line first.
